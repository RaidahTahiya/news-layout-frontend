40 Years Since Challenger Tragedy

Forty years ago, the world watched in horror as the Space Shuttle Challenger exploded in the sky over Florida, just 73 seconds after liftoff from Kennedy Space Center. The date was January 28, 1986, and what was meant to be a triumphant step forward for NASA and the nation became an indelible tragedy. All seven astronauts aboard—Francis R. Scobee, Michael J. Smith, Judith A. Resnik, Ellison S. Onizuka, Ronald E. McNair, Gregory B. Jarvis, and Christa McAuliffe, the first teacher in space—were killed. This week, as the country marks the 40th anniversary of that fateful day, the Challenger disaster remains a stark reminder of both the promise and peril of human spaceflight.

Youth Violence Prevention Programs At Risk

As Toronto’s police chief says crime rates among young people continue to be a concern, changes to federal funding programs means some of the city’s programs meant to prevent youth violence are at risk in 2026. Since 2023, the city has received a total of $16.6 million from the federal Crime Prevention Action Fund and the Building Safer Communities Fund, but both are set to end on March 31.The conclusion of the funding streams means the city’s social development department will see a $6.2-million reduction in its budget compared to last year, according to a briefing note prepared as part of the city’s ongoing 2026 budget process. City officials warn that without renewed federal support, community-based outreach, mentorship, and early-intervention programs could face cuts at a time when demand for youth services remains high.

Cold Weather Hits Ontario

Environment Canada has issued yellow-level cold weather alerts across a large portion of southern Ontario as temperatures are set to plunge. The weather agency said frigid temperatures are expected Thursday night until early Friday morning.Areas in the Toronto region are expected to feel like -30 to -35 with the child chill and slightly colder further north and east, feeling like -33 to -38 with the wind chill. Residents are being advised to limit time outdoors, dress in layers, and take precautions to prevent frostbite and hypothermia during the extreme cold.

China-Canada Trade Deal

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has hailed several new trade agreements, pledging to further diversify Ottawa’s partners while saying he “expects” the United States to respect his country’s sovereignty. Carney discussed the trade deals during a meeting on Thursday with provincial and territorial leaders. He emphasized that expanding trade relationships would help strengthen Canada’s economic resilience amid global uncertainty. Provincial leaders welcomed the agreements, saying greater market access could support growth in key sectors across the country.

Toronto Stock Exchange Plunges

Canada’s main stock index dropped nearly 1,000 points early afternoon Friday as the next nominee to lead the U.S. Federal Reserve prompted a sell-off in precious metals. “You’re seeing metals retreat for the first time, and I’d say a bit of a meaningful retreat,” said Allan Small, senior investment adviser at iA Private Wealth. The S&P/TSX composite index was down 992.37 points at 32,023.76. The April gold contract was down US$348.80 at US$5,006.00 an ounce on Friday.

Amazon Lays off 16,000 Corporate Workers

Amazon is slashing about 16,000 corporate jobs, marking the second round of mass layoffs at the e-commerce giant in the past three months. The cuts come as the company continues to scale back a workforce that expanded rapidly during the pandemic. The layoffs are part of broader cost-cutting efforts amid slowing growth and economic uncertainty. When reached, a spokesperson did not specify how many employees in Canada would be affected.

Ottawa Greenhouse Tax-Break

Alberta’s greenhouse industry says a new federal tax program could make it easier for producers to expand their operations. However, industry leaders caution that the measure is unlikely to lead to lower food prices for consumers. Ottawa announced Monday that producers who buy or build new facilities will be allowed to write off the cost of capital expansions more quickly on their taxes. The government says the change is intended to encourage investment and strengthen domestic food production.

Canada Computers Data Breach

Canada Computers & Electronics says a data breach has leaked information belonging to some of its customers. Several affected customers have expressed frustration over the limited details provided by the retailer, including how many people were impacted and when the breach occurred. The company said the breach involved personal information from its website customers, including credit card information. Canada Computers told CBC News in a statement that it became aware of the breach on Friday.
